  • Order of operations with parentheses, and word problems with money, volume and fractions

Try one together: ½ + ⅓

  1. The denominators are different, so find a common one: 6 works for both.
  2. ½ = 3/6, because 1 × 3 = 3 and 2 × 3 = 6.
  3. ⅓ = 2/6, because 1 × 2 = 2 and 3 × 2 = 6.
  4. 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6.

Tips for parents and teachers

  • For fractions, ask “What size pieces are these?” before adding. You can only add pieces of the same size.
  • With decimals, money is a great model: $2.50 + $1.75 lines up dollars and cents.
  • In order of operations, multiplication and division are partners — do them left to right, whichever comes first.

Questions parents ask

What are the hardest topics in 5th grade math?

Many kids find adding fractions with unlike denominators and long division the trickiest. Both get easier with strong times tables, which is why the quick-fact games stay in every grade.

How do you add fractions with different denominators?

Rewrite them with a common denominator, add the numerators, and keep the denominator. Banana Bread Bakery shows this step by step.
