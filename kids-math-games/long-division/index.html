What kids practice

  • The four steps: divide, multiply, subtract, bring down
  • Where to write each digit of the answer
  • Remainders: what’s left over
  • Dividing by 2-digit numbers (6th grade)

Try one together: 156 ÷ 4

  1. 4 doesn’t go into 1, so use 15: 15 ÷ 4 = 3. Write 3.
  2. Multiply and subtract: 3 × 4 = 12, and 15 − 12 = 3.
  3. Bring down the 6 to make 36. 36 ÷ 4 = 9. Write 9.
  4. So 156 ÷ 4 = 39. Check: 39 × 4 = 156 ✓

Tips for parents and teachers

  • A silly sentence helps kids remember the steps: “Does McDonald’s Sell Burgers?” — Divide, Multiply, Subtract, Bring down.
  • Most long-division mistakes are really multiplication-fact mistakes, so keep facts warm.
  • Graph paper keeps the digits lined up in neat columns.

Questions parents ask

What are the steps of long division?

Divide, multiply, subtract, bring down — then repeat until there are no more digits to bring down. Whatever is left is the remainder.

What grade is long division taught?

It starts in 4th grade with one-digit divisors, extends to two-digit divisors in 5th grade, and should be fluent by the end of 6th grade.
