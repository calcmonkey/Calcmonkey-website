What kids practice

  • Counting on and counting back on a number line (1st grade)
  • Seeing addition as moving right and subtraction as moving left
  • Where negative numbers live: to the left of zero (6th grade)
  • Adding and subtracting with negative numbers

Try one together: −3 + 5

  1. Start at −3 on the number line.
  2. Adding 5 means 5 hops to the right.
  3. −2, −1, 0, 1, 2.
  4. You land on 2, so −3 + 5 = 2.

Tips for parents and teachers

  • Draw a number line on the sidewalk with chalk and hop it for real.
  • Count the hops, not the numbers you start on — that’s the most common mistake.
  • For negatives, a thermometer is a number line standing up.

Matching school standards

These games line up with these Common Core State Standards for Mathematics:

  • 1.OA.C.5 Relate counting to addition and subtraction.
  • 2.MD.B.6 Represent whole numbers as lengths on a number line diagram.
  • 6.NS.C.6 Understand a rational number as a point on the number line, including negatives.

Questions parents ask

Why do teachers use a number line?

It turns adding and subtracting into movement kids can see — forward and backward — and later makes negative numbers, fractions and decimals easier to picture.

How do you add a negative number on a number line?

Adding a negative number moves you to the left. For example, 4 + (−6) starts at 4 and moves 6 to the left, landing on −2.
