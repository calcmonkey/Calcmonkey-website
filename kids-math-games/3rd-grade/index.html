Try one together: 23 × 4 with the area model

  1. Break 23 into 20 + 3.
  2. 20 × 4 = 80.
  3. 3 × 4 = 12.
  4. Add the parts: 80 + 12 = 92. So 23 × 4 = 92.

Tips for parents and teachers

  • Learn the “easy” tables first — 2s, 5s and 10s — then use them: 6 × 7 is 5 × 7 plus one more 7.
  • Every multiplication fact gives a division fact for free: if 6 × 7 = 42, then 42 ÷ 7 = 6.
  • Cut real food into equal pieces to talk about fractions. Equal is the important word.

Questions parents ask

Should my 3rd grader know their times tables?

Most standards expect third graders to know all products of two one-digit numbers from memory by the end of the year. A few minutes of daily practice, like the quick-fact games here, makes a big difference.

What is the area model?

It splits a multiplication into smaller boxes by place value — 23 × 4 becomes 20 × 4 and 3 × 4 — and then adds the boxes. It shows why multiplication works and leads into long multiplication later.
