What kids practice

  • Lining up numbers by place value
  • Carrying: when a column adds up to 10 or more, move the ten to the next column
  • Borrowing: when the top digit is too small, trade one from the next column
  • Lining up decimal points to add and subtract decimals (5th grade)

Try one together: 52 − 27 with borrowing

  1. Ones: 2 is smaller than 7, so borrow a ten.
  2. The 5 tens become 4 tens, and the 2 ones become 12 ones.
  3. Ones: 12 − 7 = 5. Tens: 4 − 2 = 2.
  4. So 52 − 27 = 25. Check: 25 + 27 = 52 ✓

Tips for parents and teachers

  • Always start with the ones column on the right.
  • Say what you’re doing: “I’m trading 1 ten for 10 ones” — not just “I borrow 1”.
  • Check subtraction by adding the answer back.

Questions parents ask

What is regrouping in math?

Regrouping is the newer name for carrying and borrowing: trading 10 ones for 1 ten (or 1 ten for 10 ones) so you can add or subtract column by column.

What grade learns regrouping?

It usually starts in 2nd grade with 2-digit numbers, grows to 3-digit numbers in 3rd grade, and becomes the standard method for multi-digit numbers in 4th grade.
