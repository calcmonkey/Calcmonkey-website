What kids practice

  • Parentheses first
  • Then exponents (6th grade)
  • Then multiplication and division, left to right
  • Then addition and subtraction, left to right

Try one together: 18 − (2 + 4) × 2

  1. Parentheses first: 2 + 4 = 6.
  2. Now it’s 18 − 6 × 2. Multiply next: 6 × 2 = 12.
  3. Then subtract: 18 − 12 = 6.
  4. So 18 − (2 + 4) × 2 = 6.

Tips for parents and teachers

  • PEMDAS is handy, but multiplication and division are equal partners — do whichever comes first, left to right. Same for addition and subtraction.
  • Rewrite the whole problem after every step; it prevents skipped numbers.
  • Underline the step you’re about to do before you do it.

Matching school standards

These games line up with these Common Core State Standards for Mathematics:

  • 5.OA.A.1 Use parentheses, brackets or braces in numerical expressions, and evaluate them.
  • 6.EE.A.1 Write and evaluate numerical expressions involving whole-number exponents.
  • 6.EE.A.2c Evaluate expressions in the conventional order when there are no parentheses.

Questions parents ask

What does PEMDAS stand for?

Parentheses, Exponents, Multiplication and Division, Addition and Subtraction. Some schools say BODMAS or GEMDAS — they mean the same order.

Does multiplication always come before division?

No. Multiplication and division have the same rank, so you do them from left to right in the order they appear. The same goes for addition and subtraction.
