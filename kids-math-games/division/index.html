What kids practice

  • Division facts that match the times tables
  • Division as fair sharing and as equal groups
  • Using a multiplication fact to solve a division fact
  • Long division with remainders in 4th–6th grade

Try one together: 56 ÷ 7

  1. Turn it into multiplication: 7 × what = 56?
  2. You know 7 × 8 = 56.
  3. So 56 ÷ 7 = 8.
  4. Check: 8 groups of 7 is 56 ✓

Tips for parents and teachers

  • Every division fact is a multiplication fact in disguise — practice them together.
  • Act it out: share 12 crackers among 3 people and count how many each person gets.
  • Say “how many groups of 7 fit in 56?” to make the question concrete.

Questions parents ask

How do I help my child learn division facts?

Make sure the matching multiplication facts are solid, then practice them as pairs: 6 × 4 = 24, so 24 ÷ 4 = 6 and 24 ÷ 6 = 4.

What comes after division facts?

Long division: dividing bigger numbers one digit at a time. Banana Split on this page walks through each step.
