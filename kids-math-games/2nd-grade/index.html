What kids practice

  • Addition and subtraction facts within 20, from memory
  • Place value to 1,000: hundreds, tens and ones
  • Adding and subtracting 2-digit numbers, with carrying and borrowing
  • Two-step word problems — choosing what to do first

Try one together: 46 + 38, column by column

  1. Ones first: 6 + 8 = 14.
  2. Write the 4 and carry 1 ten to the tens column.
  3. Tens: 4 + 3 + 1 (carried) = 8.
  4. So 46 + 38 = 84.

Tips for parents and teachers

  • Ask “How do you know?” after an answer. Explaining builds understanding more than speed does.
  • Use real money: dimes and pennies are a perfect model of tens and ones.
  • If borrowing is confusing, play Tens Treehouse first — trading a bunch of 10 for 10 singles is exactly what borrowing is.

Matching school standards

These games line up with these Common Core State Standards for Mathematics:

  • 2.OA.B.2 Fluently add and subtract within 20 using mental strategies; know all sums of two one-digit numbers from memory.
  • 2.NBT.A.1 Understand the three digits of a three-digit number as hundreds, tens and ones.
  • 2.NBT.B.5 Fluently add and subtract within 100 using place value.
  • 2.OA.A.1 Solve one- and two-step word problems within 100.

Questions parents ask

What math should a 2nd grader know by the end of the year?

Typically: addition facts within 20 from memory, adding and subtracting within 100 (and within 1,000 with models), place value to 1,000, telling time, counting money, and one- and two-step word problems.

Is carrying and borrowing taught in 2nd grade?

Yes. Many schools call it regrouping. Second graders usually start with 2-digit numbers, which is exactly what Column Climb practices at this grade.
