What kids practice

  • What a fraction means: equal parts of a whole
  • Comparing fractions like ⅓ and ¼
  • Equivalent fractions like ½ = 2/4 = 4/8
  • Adding and subtracting fractions with unlike denominators (5th grade)

Try one together: why does ½ = 4/8?

  1. Cut a loaf in half: 1 of 2 pieces is ½.
  2. Cut each half into 4 equal slices. Now there are 8 slices.
  3. The half you had is now 4 of those 8 slices.
  4. Same amount of bread, so ½ = 4/8.

Tips for parents and teachers

  • Always ask “are the pieces equal?” — fractions only work with equal parts.
  • Bigger denominator means smaller pieces: ⅛ of a pizza is less than ¼.
  • Cooking is fraction practice: let kids measure ½ cup and ¼ cup.

Questions parents ask

Why is ⅓ bigger than ¼?

Both are one piece, but splitting a whole into 3 equal pieces makes bigger pieces than splitting it into 4. A bigger denominator means smaller pieces.

How do you find equivalent fractions?

Multiply (or divide) the top and bottom by the same number. 2/3 × 2/2 = 4/6, so 2/3 and 4/6 are equivalent.
