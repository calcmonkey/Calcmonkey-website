What kids practice

  • Addition facts within 10 (1st grade) and within 20 (2nd grade)
  • Counting on from the bigger number
  • Doubles and near-doubles, like 6 + 7
  • Making ten to add quickly, like 9 + 5

Try one together: 7 + 6 with doubles

  1. 6 + 6 is a double: it makes 12.
  2. 7 is one more than 6.
  3. So 7 + 6 is one more than 12.
  4. 7 + 6 = 13.

Tips for parents and teachers

  • Start with the bigger number and count on: for 2 + 9, start at 9 and count 2 more.
  • Doubles (3 + 3, 4 + 4…) are easy to remember and unlock lots of other facts.
  • Turnaround facts are free: if you know 8 + 3, you know 3 + 8.

Matching school standards

These games line up with these Common Core State Standards for Mathematics:

  • 1.OA.C.6 Add and subtract within 20, with fluency within 10.
  • 2.OA.B.2 Fluently add and subtract within 20; know all sums of two one-digit numbers from memory.
  • 2.NBT.B.5 Fluently add and subtract within 100.

Questions parents ask

When should kids know addition facts by heart?

Most U.S. standards expect quick recall of facts within 10 by the end of 1st grade, and all sums of two one-digit numbers from memory by the end of 2nd grade.

What’s the best way to learn addition facts?

Learn a strategy first (counting on, doubles, making ten), then practice a little every day. Games help because kids happily do many more problems than on a worksheet.
